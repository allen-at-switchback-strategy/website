Switchback Strategy · Brand mark system

The mark, and every place it has to work.

Nine circles on one baseline — three legs of two, three and four, each peaking higher than the last — then the summit star. Green is the flow trail, blue the singletrack, navy the expert line — the red star is the Pro Line, and it is the only red on any surface it appears on.

01

Primary and compact

Primary mark
Full three-leg climb. 46px tall and up on screen; 72px in print, thread, or vinyl.
Compact mark
The same climb, same grade, reduced to one circle per tier. For square slots the primary's 2:1 won't fit — nav, avatars, app tiles, stamps. 37px on screen, 59px in print or thread.

05

Clear space and minimum size

Clear space equals the diameter of the largest circle on all four sides. Nothing crosses it — no rules, no type, no photo edges.
72px · primary, print floor
59px · compact, print floor
Floors are set by the circle, not the mark: 16px in anything physical — print, thread, vinyl, laser — and 10px on screen, where the pixel grid is finer and nothing bleeds. That gives 72px / 59px physical and 46px / 37px on screen. The compact is not the smaller mark, it is the squarer one. Below either floor, use the star alone.
06

What to protect, and what's still open

Never tighten the leg spacing
Legs sit 40 units apart against 21 within a leg — roughly double. That ratio is the only thing holding the three legs apart once the tier colors come off. An earlier draft nested them tighter and the silhouette collapsed into a sawtooth: no legs, no climb, just zigzag. Judge every change to this mark in one color first.
The peaks must keep climbing
Two, three, four circles per leg, so each leg tops one step higher than the last. If a future edit evens the legs out, the mark ascends and then plateaus — which is the failure mode we rejected. The leg lengths are the story: the same effort takes you higher each time.
72px, then switch marks
Floors follow the circle, not the mark. 16px of circle for anything physical, 10px on screen — so 72px / 59px in thread and ink, 46px / 37px in a nav bar. Anything below that is the star on its own; four elements will not survive an icon.
Guard the red
One red per surface is already the system rule, and the star should always win it. If a page has a crimson CTA, the logo goes navy or paper on that page — never two reds competing.
Wordmark weight
The reference art sets the name heavier and wider than anything else in the system. Archivo 800 at the mark's cap height keeps the trail-signage voice; anything blacker starts to feel like a sports team.
One tagline, everywhere
The tagline is "Pivot. Perfect. Ascend." — set in the design tokens and used on every surface: lockups, footers, print, and swag. Earlier drafts carried "Position" and "Protect" in the middle slot; both are retired. Where the lockup includes the tagline it sits in IBM Plex Mono, uppercase, +0.14em, never larger than the wordmark's cap height.
